### Map precipitation frames (drawn on the server)

The worker draws the Map page's precipitation (`backend/snowwatch/maps/`; `snowwatch precip`, or `snowwatch map radar|forecast|totals`, by hand) in three jobs: radar and nowcast every 6 minutes, forecast frames every 30 minutes, snow totals hourly (`map:` in `config/forecast.yaml`). Each frame is one palette PNG over a fixed western North America box, 31–57°N, 134–102°W (widened only if a location lies outside it), 1700 × 1970 px in Web Mercator, about 1.5 km a pixel and 40–60 KB a frame. Frames are listed in `data/precip/index.json` (version 2: each frame has `kind` radar / nowcast / forecast, `span_h` and `src`; there are `attribution` lines; `layers` is empty until milestone 7 of `docs/MAP-PLAN.md`) and served at `/api/precip` and `/api/precip/<name>.png`. Each job rewrites only its own part of the index, under a file lock. File names include a hash of the pixels, so browsers cache them for good; files that leave the index stay on disk 15 minutes for pages that loaded the previous one. The browser downloads the whole timeline once (~3.8 MB on 10 Nov 2026: 19 radar, 6 nowcast and 44 forecast frames) and swaps frames on one canvas, which keeps scrubbing instant and phone memory low.

A frame is only redrawn when its inputs change: a new radar scan or type picture, a new nowcast run, a new HRDPS run (`reference_time`), or a new HRRR run for that hour. Measured 10 Nov 2026 over the West box: radar cold build 54 s (peak 238 MB), a 6-minute update ~14 s (one new scan plus the six nowcast frames); forecast cold build ~90 s (peak ~350 MB).

- **Radar (past 3 h: every 12 minutes on the clock, every 6 minutes for the last 30 min):** MSC GeoMet WMS, Environment Canada's North American composite, which includes the US NEXRAD radars (1 km, every 6 minutes, 16.9–67.2°N). Re-probed 10 Nov 2026: each layer's time dimension holds 3 h at `PT6M`; one GetMap over the whole West box at 1700 × 1970 takes 0.4–1.2 s. For each time, three GetMaps:
  - `RADAR_1KM_RRAI` (rate if rain, mm/h) styled by our SLD on the `RAIN` ramp, and `RADAR_1KM_RSNO` (rate if snow, cm/h) on the `SNOW` ramp.
  - `Radar_1km_SfcPrecipType` in GeoMet's default style (`SfcPrecipType_Dis`), classified by the 12 legend colours into snow, mix (mixed rain/snow, freezing rain) and rain (rain, hail/rain). Raw values seen with GetFeatureInfo and an SLD that writes the value into the colour: 110 light snow, 120 moderate snow, 210 light mixed, 220 moderate mixed; the other classes weren't falling at the time, so colours rather than values decide (the pictures' colours differ from the legend PNG by one step in places, e.g. `#7fe0ec` vs `#7fdfeb`; both are listed). The type picture often trails the rate pictures by one scan; the newest type within 12 minutes is used.
  - Composed: snow pixels take the snow-rate colour, rain pixels the rain-rate colour, mixed pixels the `MIX` ramp by the rain rate. The type product leaves the lightest echoes untyped: on 10 Nov 2026, 26% of echo pixels over the West had no type, 83% of them under 0.5 mm/h. An untyped echo takes the nearest radar type within 8 pixels (~12 km; about 90% of them in that test), then the first forecast frame's type at that spot, else rain.
  - Radar can't see into many mountain valleys (beam blockage); the map says so with radar and nowcast frames.
- **Nowcast (next 72 min, every 12 minutes):** `Radar_1km_RainPrecipRate-Extrapolation` (mm/h) and `Radar_1km_SnowPrecipRate-Extrapolation` (cm/h), same grid, styled by our SLDs, asked for with `dim_reference_time`. Its `time` runs from its `reference_time` (which trails the newest radar scan by about 6 minutes) to 72 minutes on, every 6 minutes; only times after the newest radar scan are drawn. Echoes are moved, not grown or decayed. Typed like the radar, from the newest observed type picture.
- **Fallback radar:** RainViewer public API, `weather-maps.json`, scheme 2 with snow (`2/1_1`), tiles at zoom 7, ~2 h of history, used only when GeoMet's radar returns nothing. Its published colour table (rainviewer.com/api/color-schemes.html) gives one colour per dBZ for rain and for snow. Each pixel becomes a rate: rain via Marshall–Palmer (Z = 200R^1.6), snow via Z = 180S^2.
- **Forecast, Canada and northern US (hourly, ~48 h):** MSC GeoMet WMS (`geo.weather.gc.ca/geomet`), HRDPS 2.5 km, `HRDPS.CONTINENTAL.DIAG_{RN,FR,SN}_PT1H` (mm in the hour ending at `time`). One GetMap per layer for the whole box, styled by an SLD in `sld_body` (each `ColorMapEntry` is a band's lower bound; below the first is transparent). Only one layer per request. The domain reaches about 42–44°N in the West. Free, no key, Open Government Licence – Canada.
- **Forecast, rest of the US:** clipped to outside the HRDPS domain (mask = `DIAG_PR_PT1H` styled solid).
  - NOAA HRRR simulated radar by type, from Iowa State's IEM tile cache, `cache/tile.py/1.0.0/hrrr::REFP-F{minute}-0/{z}/{x}/{y}.png`. Each minute slot comes from the newest run reaching it (hourly runs to 18 h, 6-hourly to 48 h). `data/gis/images/4326/hrrr/refp_{minute}.json` gives its valid time and run. Missing slots answer slowly with 503, so tiles aren't retried and one probe tile decides. IEM's palette families (greens/yellows rain, blues snow, purples/reds ice) map onto ours by lightness.
  - Where HRRR has nothing: NWS WPC 6-hour QPF, `mapservices.weather.noaa.gov/vector/rest/services/precip/wpc_qpf/MapServer` layers 13–25 (hours 0–6 … 72–78 from layer 13's `start_time`, UTC), fetched as GeoJSON and drawn in tan, with no type.
  - Not used: the NDFD WMS (days stale when checked, Nov 2026).
- **The map itself:** MapLibre GL (WebGL), so it can tilt, turn and show 3D terrain.
  - Base map: Esri World Topo up to zoom 12, then a half-level cross-fade to OpenTopoMap (contours, trails, peaks, lifts), dimmed in the dark theme.
  - Ski runs, lifts and peaks: OpenStreetMap via Overpass, asked for `piste:type` ways, `aerialway` ways and named `natural=peak` nodes in a ~35 km box around each location. The worker checks hourly and fetches again weekly (six hours if it got nothing) or when the locations change, one query per location, trying overpass-api.de, maps.mail.ru, overpass.private.coffee and overpass.kumi.systems in turn (`backend/snowwatch/osm.py`, or `snowwatch ski-map` by hand), saving one GeoJSON file, `data/osm/ski.geojson`, served at `/api/osm/ski.geojson`. The map draws them as lines coloured by difficulty (touring and backcountry dashed) with names along the runs, from zoom 11, plus peak names and heights. Touring lines are only as complete as OSM's mappers have made them. Label fonts (MapLibre's demo glyphs, `Open Sans Semibold` and `Noto Sans Regular`) come through the server at `/api/glyphs/…`. The OpenSnowMap picture overlay is no longer drawn, though the tile cache still serves it.
  - Base map tiles come through the server's tile cache, `/api/tiles/{esri,otm}/{z}/{x}/{y}.png` (`backend/snowwatch/tiles.py`, files in `data/tiles/`, unused ones pruned nightly after 45 days, and the cache capped at TILES_MAX_MB). OpenTopoMap takes ~0.5–1 s a tile and OpenSnowMap ~0.5 s, so a repeat view is far quicker from the cache. Esri's tiles have a lighter last row and column; the cache repairs them once, so the map has no grid.
  - Satellite style: Esri World Imagery (to zoom 18) with Esri's Boundaries & Places labels on top, also through the tile cache (`sat`, `satlabels`).
  - 3D terrain: AWS/Mapzen terrain tiles (`elevation-tiles-prod/terrarium`, CORS open, no key), up to zoom 15 (~3 m a pixel around Snoqualmie; zoom 16 doesn't exist), exaggerated ×1.15. Fetched straight from AWS, since they're fast and large.
  - Snow totals ("Snow total" on the map): MSC GeoMet WCS 2.0.1, layer `HRDPS.CONTINENTAL_SN` (water-equivalent total since the run began, mm), fetched as float GeoTIFFs for this hour and 12, 24 and 48 h on (or the run's end) and subtracted. At 10:1, mm of water is cm of snow. Drawn on whole-inch steps (1, 3, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24, 36, 48 in) as `t<hours>-<hash>.png` beside the frames and listed under `totals` in the index. Covers Canada and the northern US only.
  - Precipitation is a MapLibre canvas source over the frames' box, uploaded once per frame shown. It drapes over the terrain and fades out from zoom 11 to 13.
  - Offline maps (Map → download button): saves, per location, OpenTopoMap tiles to zoom 15 and AWS terrain to zoom 14 (15 within 4 km) for ~16 × 16 km, plus the ski-run file and label fonts, in the browser's cache (`frontend/src/lib/offlineMaps.ts`; the service worker answers map requests from it first). About 35–50 MB a location. Esri's topo and satellite tiles aren't saved: Esri's terms prohibit "systematically requesting ArcGIS tiles for offline use through other apps or services", so offline the map shows OpenTopoMap at every zoom. OpenTopoMap asks not to be overloaded by mass downloads, so areas are capped at zoom 15 and fetched three at a time through the server's tile cache.
  - Your location: the browser's GPS (and compass, for the direction cone), shown only on the device; nothing is sent to the server.
  - Phones draw at 2× rather than 3× and keep fewer tiles in memory. In headless Chrome on an iPhone profile, the page held ~220 MB through playback and panning in 3D.
- **NOAA GRIB2 by byte range (ready for milestone 2):** `backend/snowwatch/maps/grib.py` reads a file's `.idx`, fetches one message with an HTTP Range request (only a 206 answer is accepted) and decodes it with `gribberish` 1.9 (MIT; wheels for x86-64, ARM64 and ARMv7 Linux; needs only NumPy). Re-probed 10 Nov 2026 on `noaa-hrrr-bdp-pds`: `hrrr.t14z.wrfsfcf01.grib2.idx` lists `APCP:surface:0-1 hour acc fcst` (218 KB) and `CSNOW`, `CICEP`, `CFRZR`, `CRAIN` (`1 hour fcst`, 1–30 KB each; `CSNOW` is 5.7 KB, kept as a test fixture). `CSNOW` decoded in 12 ms to a 1059 × 1799 Lambert conformal grid; `metadata.latlng()` returns flat arrays (south row first, longitudes −180…180) in 0.12 s. Mapping that grid onto the West box (nearest cell per pixel) takes 0.3 s and is cached as `.npy` per grid and box. Our own HRRR replaces the IEM/WPC part in milestone 2.
