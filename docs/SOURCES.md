### Map precipitation frames (drawn on the server)

The worker draws the Map page's precipitation (`backend/snowwatch/maps/`; `snowwatch precip`, or `snowwatch map radar|forecast|totals`, by hand) in three jobs: radar and nowcast every 6 minutes, forecast frames (hourly to ~48 h, then 3-hourly to ~84 h and 6-hourly to 10 days) every 30 minutes, snow totals (12 h to 10 days) hourly (`map:` in `config/forecast.yaml`). Each frame is one palette PNG over a fixed western North America box, 31–57°N, 134–102°W (widened only if a location lies outside it), 1700 × 1970 px in Web Mercator, about 1.5 km a pixel and 40–60 KB a frame. Frames are listed in `data/precip/index.json` (version 2: each frame has `kind` radar / nowcast / forecast, `span_h` and `src`; there are `attribution` lines; `layers` is empty until milestone 7 of `docs/MAP-PLAN.md`) and served at `/api/precip` and `/api/precip/<name>.png`. Each job rewrites only its own part of the index, under a file lock. File names include a hash of the pixels, so browsers cache them for good; files that leave the index stay on disk 15 minutes for pages that loaded the previous one. The browser downloads the timeline to ~48 h straight away (radar, nowcast and 44 hourly frames: 2.7 MB of forecast frames on 10 Nov 2026) and the 3- and 6-hour frames beyond (37 frames, 1.6 MB) when the slider reaches them or after 5 idle seconds, and swaps frames on one canvas, which keeps scrubbing instant and phone memory low. Working files the browser never sees live in `data/maps/`: `grids/` (each model grid's pixel map, ~26 MB), `grib/` (NBM messages, kept two days, ~70 MB per run) and `totals/` (each window's snow total as float16 cm, ~45 MB).

A frame is only redrawn when its inputs change: a new radar scan or type picture, a new nowcast run, a new HRDPS, RDPS or GDPS run (`reference_time`), a new HRRR run for that hour, or a new NBM run. Measured 10 Nov 2026 over the West box: radar cold build 54 s (peak 238 MB), a 6-minute update ~14 s (one new scan plus the six nowcast frames); forecast cold build to 10 days ~150 s (peak ~375 MB); snow totals ~50 s (peak ~410 MB, ~90 WCS requests).

- **Radar (past 3 h: every 12 minutes on the clock, every 6 minutes for the last 30 min):** MSC GeoMet WMS, Environment Canada's North American composite, which includes the US NEXRAD radars (1 km, every 6 minutes, 16.9–67.2°N). Re-probed 10 Nov 2026: each layer's time dimension holds 3 h at `PT6M`; one GetMap over the whole West box at 1700 × 1970 takes 0.4–1.2 s. For each time, three GetMaps:
  - `RADAR_1KM_RRAI` (rate if rain, mm/h) styled by our SLD on the `RAIN` ramp, and `RADAR_1KM_RSNO` (rate if snow, cm/h) on the `SNOW` ramp.
  - `Radar_1km_SfcPrecipType` in GeoMet's default style (`SfcPrecipType_Dis`), classified by the 12 legend colours into snow, mix (mixed rain/snow, freezing rain) and rain (rain, hail/rain). Raw values seen with GetFeatureInfo and an SLD that writes the value into the colour: 110 light snow, 120 moderate snow, 210 light mixed, 220 moderate mixed; the other classes weren't falling at the time, so colours rather than values decide (the pictures' colours differ from the legend PNG by one step in places, e.g. `#7fe0ec` vs `#7fdfeb`; both are listed). The type picture often trails the rate pictures by one scan; the newest type within 12 minutes is used.
  - Composed: snow pixels take the snow-rate colour, rain pixels the rain-rate colour, mixed pixels the `MIX` ramp by the rain rate. The type product leaves the lightest echoes untyped: on 10 Nov 2026, 26% of echo pixels over the West had no type, 83% of them under 0.5 mm/h. An untyped echo takes the nearest radar type within 8 pixels (~12 km; about 90% of them in that test), then the first forecast frame's type at that spot, else rain.
  - Radar can't see into many mountain valleys (beam blockage); the map says so with radar and nowcast frames.
- **Nowcast (next 72 min, every 12 minutes):** `Radar_1km_RainPrecipRate-Extrapolation` (mm/h) and `Radar_1km_SnowPrecipRate-Extrapolation` (cm/h), same grid, styled by our SLDs, asked for with `dim_reference_time`. Its `time` runs from its `reference_time` (which trails the newest radar scan by about 6 minutes) to 72 minutes on, every 6 minutes; only times after the newest radar scan are drawn. Echoes are moved, not grown or decayed. Typed like the radar, from the newest observed type picture.
- **Fallback radar:** RainViewer public API, `weather-maps.json`, scheme 2 with snow (`2/1_1`), tiles at zoom 7, ~2 h of history, used only when GeoMet's radar returns nothing. Its published colour table (rainviewer.com/api/color-schemes.html) gives one colour per dBZ for rain and for snow. Each pixel becomes a rate: rain via Marshall–Palmer (Z = 200R^1.6), snow via Z = 180S^2.
- **Forecast, Canada and northern US (hourly, ~48 h):** MSC GeoMet WMS (`geo.weather.gc.ca/geomet`), HRDPS 2.5 km, `HRDPS.CONTINENTAL.DIAG_{RN,FR,SN}_PT1H` (mm in the hour ending at `time`). One GetMap per layer for the whole box, styled by an SLD in `sld_body` (each `ColorMapEntry` is a band's lower bound; below the first is transparent). Only one layer per request. The domain reaches about 42–44°N in the West. Free, no key, Open Government Licence – Canada.
- **Forecast, rest of the US (hourly, ~48 h):** clipped to outside the HRDPS domain (mask = `DIAG_PR_PT1H` styled solid).
  - NOAA HRRR 3 km from NOAA's own GRIB2 files on AWS (`noaa-hrrr-bdp-pds`, `hrrr.YYYYMMDD/conus/hrrr.tHHz.wrfsfcfNN.grib2`), by byte range (`maps/sources/hrrr.py`). Each valid hour comes from the newest run that has it: an S3 listing per candidate run (hourly runs to 18 h, the 00/06/12/18 UTC runs to 48 h) shows which forecast hours are published. Per hour: `APCP:surface:{N-1}-{N} hour acc fcst` (the hour's own amount; at N ≥ 2 the file also holds `0-N`, so the pattern must name the hour) and the type flags `CSNOW`, `CRAIN`, `CFRZR`, `CICEP` (`N hour fcst`, the end of the hour). Snow and rain together, freezing rain and ice pellets → mix. A wet pixel without a flag takes the nearest flag within 8 px, else rain. ~0.3 MB and ~0.15 s decode per hour. HRRR's grid stops at about 134°W at 48°N and 122.7°W at 21°N, so the box's south-west corner (open Pacific) is clear.
  - Hours no HRRR run reaches: NBM's 1-hour `APCP` and `ASNOW` (below).
- **Forecast beyond ~48 h (`span_h` 3 and 6):** north of 49°N the Canadian models, south of it NBM, joined hard at 49°N (milestone 2's last task, a blend in data space, replaces this; see MAP-PLAN decision 8). Colours always mean the average hourly rate.
  - 3-hourly to the RDPS run's end (~84 h): `RDPS_10km_{Rain,Snow}-Accum3h` and `RDPS_10km_{FreezingRain,IcePellets}-Accum-3h` (note the extra hyphen; `RDPS_10km_FreezingRain-Accum3h` doesn't exist). Re-probed 10 Nov 2026: the time dimension is 3-hourly (`PT3H`, not hourly as first noted), run+3 h to run+84 h, runs every 6 h; values mm of water; WCS returns a coarse 0.09° × 0.32° grid for this rotated-grid model.
  - 6-hourly to the GDPS run's end (240 h): `GDPS_15km_{Rain,Snow}-Accum6h`, `GDPS_15km_{FreezingRain,IcePellets}-Accum-6h`, on 00/06/12/18 UTC, runs every 12 h.
  - Both drawn by our SLD with the ramp's bounds × 3 or × 6, asked for with `dim_reference_time`, layered rain → ice pellets → freezing rain → snow on top. Both models cover all of North America, so they also fill the US wherever NBM has nothing.
  - NOAA NBM 2.5 km on AWS (`noaa-nbm-grib2-pds`, `blend.YYYYMMDD/HH/core/blend.tHHz.core.fNNN.co.grib2`, `maps/sources/nbm.py`). Re-probed 10 Nov 2026, and different from the plan's first reading: only the 00/06/12/18 UTC runs reach 264 h (hourly files to 48 h, 3-hourly to 192 h, 6-hourly to 264 h); the 3-hourly files between 48 and 192 h hold only a 1-hour `APCP` (no 3-hour sums, no `ASNOW`); `APCP` and `ASNOW` over 6 hours are in the file at every 6th hour; 1-hour `ASNOW` exists to 48 h only. The 01/07/13/19 UTC runs have APCP-only files to 264 h, unused. So beyond 48 h NBM works in 6-hour blocks, and a 3-hour frame shows its block's average. A run counts once its f264 `.idx` exists; `map.nbm_cycles` (default 00 and 12 UTC) picks which runs. `ASNOW` is metres of snow with NBM's own ratio, `APCP` mm. Type from the share of water falling as snow at 10:1: ≥ 0.6 snow, ≥ 0.2 mix, else rain. Probability and percentile messages share the plain message's name in the `.idx` (e.g. `APCP:surface:234-240 hour acc fcst:prob >0.254:…`), so `grib.find` skips any line with fields after the forecast time. NBM's grid (1597 × 2345 Lambert, 19–57°N, 138–59°W) covers the whole box. **Its rows alternate direction** (GRIB scanning mode `0x50`, bit `0x10`); `gribberish` returns them as stored, so every other row came out mirrored until `grib.decode` undid it (`unsnake`). Messages are 0.6–1.2 MB and cached on disk.
  - Not used: the NDFD WMS (days stale when checked, Nov 2026).
- **The map itself:** MapLibre GL (WebGL), so it can tilt, turn and show 3D terrain.
  - Base map: Esri World Topo up to zoom 12, then a half-level cross-fade to OpenTopoMap (contours, trails, peaks, lifts), dimmed in the dark theme.
  - Ski runs, lifts and peaks: OpenStreetMap via Overpass, asked for `piste:type` ways, `aerialway` ways and named `natural=peak` nodes in a ~35 km box around each location. The worker checks hourly and fetches again weekly (six hours if it got nothing) or when the locations change, one query per location, trying overpass-api.de, maps.mail.ru, overpass.private.coffee and overpass.kumi.systems in turn (`backend/snowwatch/osm.py`, or `snowwatch ski-map` by hand), saving one GeoJSON file, `data/osm/ski.geojson`, served at `/api/osm/ski.geojson`. The map draws them as lines coloured by difficulty (touring and backcountry dashed) with names along the runs, from zoom 11, plus peak names and heights. Touring lines are only as complete as OSM's mappers have made them. Label fonts (MapLibre's demo glyphs, `Open Sans Semibold` and `Noto Sans Regular`) come through the server at `/api/glyphs/…`. The OpenSnowMap picture overlay is no longer drawn, though the tile cache still serves it.
  - Base map tiles come through the server's tile cache, `/api/tiles/{esri,otm}/{z}/{x}/{y}.png` (`backend/snowwatch/tiles.py`, files in `data/tiles/`, unused ones pruned nightly after 45 days, and the cache capped at TILES_MAX_MB). OpenTopoMap takes ~0.5–1 s a tile and OpenSnowMap ~0.5 s, so a repeat view is far quicker from the cache. Esri's tiles have a lighter last row and column; the cache repairs them once, so the map has no grid.
  - Satellite style: Esri World Imagery (to zoom 18) with Esri's Boundaries & Places labels on top, also through the tile cache (`sat`, `satlabels`).
  - 3D terrain: AWS/Mapzen terrain tiles (`elevation-tiles-prod/terrarium`, CORS open, no key), up to zoom 15 (~3 m a pixel around Snoqualmie; zoom 16 doesn't exist), exaggerated ×1.15. Fetched straight from AWS, since they're fast and large.
  - Snow totals ("Snow total" on the map, `maps/totals.py`): windows of 12, 24 and 48 h, 3, 5, 7 and 10 days from the top of this hour. Windows over two days end on the 00/06/12/18 UTC hour before, where NBM's 6-hour sums end, and are dropped if the models stop before 90% of the window (the 10-day one ends where GDPS or NBM stops, ~230 h). Index entries have `window` (asked), `hours` (actual), `source` and `runs` (each model's run start).
    - North of 49°N: MSC GeoMet WCS 2.0.1 float GeoTIFFs of the snow water since the run began (mm), chained by lead time `HRDPS.CONTINENTAL_SN` (to 48 h) → `RDPS_10km_Snow-Accum` (to 84 h, hourly) → `GDPS_15km_Snow-Accum` (hourly to 84 h, 3-hourly to 240 h), each step a difference within one run. WCS honours `DIM_REFERENCE_TIME` (checked: two GDPS runs gave different grids for the same time), so every request is pinned to its run. Steps end on 00/06/12/18 UTC; each step's water becomes depth with the `slr_bands` ratio (8–17:1) from the 2 m temperature at its end (`HRDPS.CONTINENTAL_TT`, `RDPS_10km_AirTemp_2m`, `GDPS_15km_AirTemp_2m`, °C), 10:1 where missing.
    - South of 49°N: NBM `ASNOW` summed (1-hour sums to the first 6-hour boundary, then 6-hour blocks; a block partly inside a window counts in proportion).
    - Joined by a linear blend from 48.5 to 49.5°N; either side fills in where the other has no data. Drawn on whole-inch steps (1, 3, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24, 36, 48 in) as `t<hours>-<hash>.png`, and kept as numbers in `data/maps/totals/<window>h.npy` (float16 cm) for tap-for-numbers and terrain adjustment later.
  - Precipitation is a MapLibre canvas source over the frames' box, uploaded once per frame shown. It drapes over the terrain and fades out from zoom 11 to 13.
  - Offline maps (Map → download button): saves, per location, OpenTopoMap tiles to zoom 15 and AWS terrain to zoom 14 (15 within 4 km) for ~16 × 16 km, plus the ski-run file and label fonts, in the browser's cache (`frontend/src/lib/offlineMaps.ts`; the service worker answers map requests from it first). About 35–50 MB a location. Esri's topo and satellite tiles aren't saved: Esri's terms prohibit "systematically requesting ArcGIS tiles for offline use through other apps or services", so offline the map shows OpenTopoMap at every zoom. OpenTopoMap asks not to be overloaded by mass downloads, so areas are capped at zoom 15 and fetched three at a time through the server's tile cache.
  - Your location: the browser's GPS (and compass, for the direction cone), shown only on the device; nothing is sent to the server.
  - Phones draw at 2× rather than 3× and keep fewer tiles in memory. In headless Chrome on an iPhone profile, the page held ~220 MB through playback and panning in 3D.
- **NOAA GRIB2 by byte range:** `backend/snowwatch/maps/grib.py` reads a file's `.idx`, fetches one message with an HTTP Range request (only a 206 answer is accepted) and decodes it with `gribberish` 1.9 (MIT; wheels for x86-64, ARM64 and ARMv7 Linux; needs only NumPy). Re-probed 10 Nov 2026 on `noaa-hrrr-bdp-pds`: `hrrr.t14z.wrfsfcf01.grib2.idx` lists `APCP:surface:0-1 hour acc fcst` (218 KB) and `CSNOW`, `CICEP`, `CFRZR`, `CRAIN` (`1 hour fcst`, 1–30 KB each; `CSNOW` is 5.7 KB, kept as a test fixture). `CSNOW` decoded in 12 ms to a 1059 × 1799 Lambert conformal grid; `metadata.latlng()` returns flat arrays (south row first, longitudes −180…180) in 0.12 s. Mapping that grid onto the West box (nearest cell per pixel) takes 0.3 s and is cached as `.npy` per grid and box. `fetch_messages` reads the `.idx` once for several messages of one file and can cache them on disk. Milestone 2 removed the IEM HRRR tiles and the WPC polygons.
