### Map precipitation frames (drawn on the server)

The worker draws the Map page's precipitation (`backend/snowwatch/maps/`; `snowwatch precip`, or `snowwatch map radar|forecast|totals`, by hand) in three jobs: radar and nowcast every 6 minutes, forecast frames (hourly to ~48 h, then 3-hourly to ~84 h and 6-hourly to 10 days) every 30 minutes, snow totals (12 h to 10 days) hourly (`map:` in `config/forecast.yaml`). Each frame is one palette PNG over a fixed western North America box, 31–57°N, 134–102°W (widened only if a location lies outside it), 1700 × 1970 px in Web Mercator, about 1.5 km a pixel and 40–60 KB a frame. Frames are listed in `data/precip/index.json` (version 2: each frame has `kind` radar / nowcast / forecast, `span_h` and `src`; there are `attribution` lines; `layers` is empty until milestone 7 of `docs/MAP-PLAN.md`) and served at `/api/precip` and `/api/precip/<name>.png`. Each job rewrites only its own part of the index, under a file lock. File names include a hash of the pixels, so browsers cache them for good; files that leave the index stay on disk 15 minutes for pages that loaded the previous one. The browser downloads the timeline to ~48 h straight away (radar, nowcast and 44 hourly frames: 2.7 MB of forecast frames on 10 Nov 2026) and the 3- and 6-hour frames beyond (37 frames, 1.6 MB) when the slider reaches them or after 5 idle seconds, and swaps frames on one canvas, which keeps scrubbing instant and phone memory low. Working files the browser never sees live in `data/maps/`: `grids/` (each model grid's pixel map and HRDPS's domain, ~30 MB), `grib/` (NBM and HRRR messages, ~70 MB per NBM run plus ~0.3 MB per HRRR hour), `wcs/` (GeoMet grids by model run, ~30 MB) and `totals/` (each window's snow total as float16 cm, ~45 MB). Cached downloads are dropped a day after they were last used.

A frame is only redrawn when its inputs change: a new radar scan or type picture, a new nowcast run, a new HRDPS, RDPS or GDPS run (`reference_time`), a new HRRR run for that hour, or a new NBM run. Measured 10 Nov 2026 over the West box: radar cold build 54 s (peak 238 MB), a 6-minute update ~14 s (one new scan plus the six nowcast frames); forecast cold build to 10 days ~180 s (peak ~570 MB with two frames in work at once), ~85 s when only one model's run changed and the rest comes from the caches; snow totals ~45 s cold, ~17 s from the caches (peak ~400 MB).

- **Radar (past 3 h: every 12 minutes on the clock, every 6 minutes for the last 30 min):** MSC GeoMet WMS, Environment Canada's North American composite, which includes the US NEXRAD radars (1 km, every 6 minutes, 16.9–67.2°N). Re-probed 10 Nov 2026: each layer's time dimension holds 3 h at `PT6M`; one GetMap over the whole West box at 1700 × 1970 takes 0.4–1.2 s. For each time, three GetMaps:
  - `RADAR_1KM_RRAI` (rate if rain, mm/h) styled by our SLD on the `RAIN` ramp, and `RADAR_1KM_RSNO` (rate if snow, cm/h) on the `SNOW` ramp.
  - `Radar_1km_SfcPrecipType` in GeoMet's default style (`SfcPrecipType_Dis`), classified by the 12 legend colours into snow, mix (mixed rain/snow, freezing rain) and rain (rain, hail/rain). Raw values seen with GetFeatureInfo and an SLD that writes the value into the colour: 110 light snow, 120 moderate snow, 210 light mixed, 220 moderate mixed; the other classes weren't falling at the time, so colours rather than values decide (the pictures' colours differ from the legend PNG by one step in places, e.g. `#7fe0ec` vs `#7fdfeb`; both are listed). The type picture often trails the rate pictures by one scan; the newest type within 12 minutes is used.
  - Composed: snow pixels take the snow-rate colour, rain pixels the rain-rate colour, mixed pixels the `MIX` ramp by the rain rate. The type product leaves the lightest echoes untyped: on 10 Nov 2026, 26% of echo pixels over the West had no type, 83% of them under 0.5 mm/h. An untyped echo takes the nearest radar type within 8 pixels (~12 km; about 90% of them in that test), then the first forecast frame's type at that spot, else rain.
  - Radar can't see into many mountain valleys (beam blockage); the map says so with radar and nowcast frames.
- **Nowcast (next 72 min, every 12 minutes):** `Radar_1km_RainPrecipRate-Extrapolation` (mm/h) and `Radar_1km_SnowPrecipRate-Extrapolation` (cm/h), same grid, styled by our SLDs, asked for with `dim_reference_time`. Its `time` runs from its `reference_time` (which trails the newest radar scan by about 6 minutes) to 72 minutes on, every 6 minutes; only times after the newest radar scan are drawn. Echoes are moved, not grown or decayed. Typed like the radar, from the newest observed type picture.
- **Fallback radar:** RainViewer public API, `weather-maps.json`, scheme 2 with snow (`2/1_1`), tiles at zoom 7, ~2 h of history, used only when GeoMet's radar returns nothing. Its published colour table (rainviewer.com/api/color-schemes.html) gives one colour per dBZ for rain and for snow. Each pixel becomes a rate: rain via Marshall–Palmer (Z = 200R^1.6), snow via Z = 180S^2.
- **How sources are joined (`maps/blend.py`):** as numbers, not pictures. Each source is put on the box's grid as an hourly rate (mm of water) and a type code; colours are applied once, afterwards. Each source has a weight per pixel: 1 well inside its domain, easing to 0 over about 100 km (67 px) at its edge (`feather`: the share of valid pixels in a square window, so ½ on a straight edge and 1 once the window is inside; the box's own edge doesn't count as an edge). Sources are laid down in order of preference, each taking its weight of whatever is left (`mosaic`), so there is no averaging of two models inside the preferred one, only a cross-fade in its last ~100 km. Amounts are the weighted mean; the type comes from the source with the largest weight that has a type there. Every source types any amount down to 0.01 mm, so a pixel that is only wet once sources are mixed still finds a type; a wet pixel with none takes the nearest within 8 px, else rain.
- **Forecast, Canada and northern US (hourly, ~48 h):** MSC GeoMet (`geo.weather.gc.ca/geomet`), HRDPS 2.5 km, `HRDPS.CONTINENTAL.DIAG_{RN,SN,FR,PE}_PT1H` (mm in the hour ending at `time`: rain, snow as water, freezing rain, ice pellets), read as raw grids by WCS 2.0.1 rather than styled pictures. Probed 10–11 Nov 2026:
  - A whole-box grid is 804 × 748 float32 (0.044° × 0.034°), 2.4 MB as sent. GeoMet doesn't gzip it, but `geotiff:compression=Deflate` in the request gives the same values in ~0.6 MB for a wet hour and a few KB for a dry layer (LZW and PackBits also work, larger). Pillow reads it. `scalefactor` and `SCALESIZE` work too but lose the model's detail, so they aren't used.
  - **The grids hold 0, not "no data", outside HRDPS's domain** (it reaches about 42–44°N in the West), so the domain comes from a WMS picture of `DIAG_PR_PT1H` styled solid, fetched when frames need drawing and kept in `data/maps/grids/` for when GeoMet won't draw it. Without either, the hourly frames are left as they are.
  - Requests carry `DIM_REFERENCE_TIME` and answers are cached by run in `data/maps/wcs/`: an hour's HRDPS grids are wanted again each time a newer HRRR run covers that hour.
  - The type comes from how the water splits: snow if ≥ 60% falls as snow, mix if ≥ 20% does or ≥ 30% is freezing rain or ice pellets, else rain (the same thresholds as NBM below). Amounts are interpolated between cells (bilinear). Interpolating from a snow cell to a rain cell passes through every snow share, which rings each snow area with "mix" the model never forecast, so mix is kept only next to a cell that is itself mixed, and elsewhere the snow/rain line falls where the snow share crosses a half.
  - Free, no key, Open Government Licence – Canada.
- **Forecast, rest of the US (hourly, ~48 h):** HRDPS is preferred across its domain and fades over its last ~100 km into HRRR.
  - NOAA HRRR 3 km from NOAA's own GRIB2 files on AWS (`noaa-hrrr-bdp-pds`, `hrrr.YYYYMMDD/conus/hrrr.tHHz.wrfsfcfNN.grib2`), by byte range (`maps/sources/hrrr.py`). Each valid hour comes from the newest run that has it: an S3 listing per candidate run (hourly runs to 18 h, the 00/06/12/18 UTC runs to 48 h) shows which forecast hours are published. Per hour: `APCP:surface:{N-1}-{N} hour acc fcst` (the hour's own amount; at N ≥ 2 the file also holds `0-N`, so the pattern must name the hour) and the type flags `CSNOW`, `CRAIN`, `CFRZR`, `CICEP` (`N hour fcst`, the end of the hour). Snow and rain together, freezing rain and ice pellets → mix. A wet pixel without a flag takes the nearest flag within 8 px, else rain. ~0.3 MB and ~0.15 s decode per hour. HRRR's grid stops at about 134°W at 48°N and 122.7°W at 21°N, so the box's south-west corner (open Pacific) comes from RDPS (below). Messages are cached in `data/maps/grib/`.
  - Hours no HRRR run reaches: NBM's 1-hour `APCP` and `ASNOW` (below).
  - Where neither HRDPS nor the US model has data (over the default box, the Pacific south-west of HRRR's edge, where storms come from): RDPS hour by hour, `RDPS_10km_{Rain,Snow}-Accum1h` and `RDPS_10km_{FreezingRain,IcePellets}-Accum-1h` (hourly to 84 h, tiny grids). It covers everything, so no pixel of an hourly frame is without a source. Frames it helped carry `src.fill: "rdps"`.
- **Forecast beyond ~48 h (`span_h` 3 and 6):** the Canadian models in Canada, NBM in the US. NBM's weight is its domain weight times a preference that is 1 south of 48.5°N and 0 north of 49.5°N (the border is 49°N across the whole box); the Canadian model takes the rest, including wherever NBM's grid ends. Colours always mean the average hourly rate.
  - 3-hourly to the RDPS run's end (~84 h): `RDPS_10km_{Rain,Snow}-Accum3h` and `RDPS_10km_{FreezingRain,IcePellets}-Accum-3h` (note the extra hyphen; `RDPS_10km_FreezingRain-Accum3h` doesn't exist). Re-probed 10 Nov 2026: the time dimension is 3-hourly (`PT3H`, not hourly as first noted), run+3 h to run+84 h, runs every 6 h; values mm of water; WCS returns a coarse 0.09° × 0.32° grid for this rotated-grid model.
  - 6-hourly to the GDPS run's end (240 h): `GDPS_15km_{Rain,Snow}-Accum6h`, `GDPS_15km_{FreezingRain,IcePellets}-Accum-6h`, on 00/06/12/18 UTC, runs every 12 h.
  - Both read as WCS grids like HRDPS (four layers a frame, each a few KB to 100 KB), pinned to their run, interpolated between cells so 10–15 km models don't come out as blocks. Both cover all of North America, so they also fill the US wherever NBM has nothing.
  - **A 3-hour frame's NBM share:** NBM has only 6-hour sums beyond 48 h, so the 3 hours ending at `t` take `(a + 0.25) / (a + b + 0.5)` of NBM's 6-hour amount, where `a` and `b` are RDPS's precipitation (`RDPS_10km_Precip-Accum3h`, mm) in this half and the other half of the same six hours. The two halves' shares add to 1, so NBM's amount is neither lost nor counted twice; where RDPS is dry the split is even. The 0.25 mm keeps a trace in one half from taking everything.
  - NOAA NBM 2.5 km on AWS (`noaa-nbm-grib2-pds`, `blend.YYYYMMDD/HH/core/blend.tHHz.core.fNNN.co.grib2`, `maps/sources/nbm.py`). Re-probed 10 Nov 2026, and different from the plan's first reading: only the 00/06/12/18 UTC runs reach 264 h (hourly files to 48 h, 3-hourly to 192 h, 6-hourly to 264 h); the 3-hourly files between 48 and 192 h hold only a 1-hour `APCP` (no 3-hour sums, no `ASNOW`); `APCP` and `ASNOW` over 6 hours are in the file at every 6th hour; 1-hour `ASNOW` exists to 48 h only. The 01/07/13/19 UTC runs have APCP-only files to 264 h, unused. So beyond 48 h NBM works in 6-hour blocks (the block ending at hour 48 included). A run counts once its f264 `.idx` exists; `map.nbm_cycles` (default 00 and 12 UTC) picks which runs. `ASNOW` is metres of snow with NBM's own ratio, `APCP` mm. Type from the share of water falling as snow at 10:1: ≥ 0.6 snow, ≥ 0.2 mix, else rain. Probability and percentile messages share the plain message's name in the `.idx` (e.g. `APCP:surface:234-240 hour acc fcst:prob >0.254:…`), so `grib.find` skips any line with fields after the forecast time. NBM's grid (1597 × 2345 Lambert, 19–57°N, 138–59°W) covers the whole box. **Its rows alternate direction** (GRIB scanning mode `0x50`, bit `0x10`); `gribberish` returns them as stored, so every other row came out mirrored until `grib.decode` undid it (`unsnake`). Messages are 0.6–1.2 MB and cached on disk.
  - Not used: the NDFD WMS (days stale when checked, Nov 2026).

Drawn on whole-inch steps (1, 3, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24, 36, 48 in) as `t<hours>-<hash>.png`, and kept as numbers in `data/maps/totals/<window>h.npy` (float16 cm) for tap-for-numbers and terrain adjustment later.
  - Precipitation is a MapLibre canvas source over the frames' box, uploaded once per frame shown. It drapes over the terrain and fades out from zoom 11 to 13.
  - Offline maps (Map → download button): saves, per location, OpenTopoMap tiles to zoom 15 and Mapterhorn elevation to zoom 13 (14 within 4 km; a 404 is skipped, not a failure) for ~16 × 16 km, plus the ski-run file and label fonts, in the browser's cache (`frontend/src/lib/offlineMaps.ts`; the service worker answers map requests from it first). About 35–50 MB a location. Esri's topo and satellite tiles aren't saved: Esri's terms prohibit "systematically requesting ArcGIS tiles for offline use through other apps or services", so offline the map shows OpenTopoMap at every zoom. OpenTopoMap asks not to be overloaded by mass downloads, so areas are capped at zoom 15 and fetched three at a time through the server's tile cache.
  - Your location: the browser's GPS (and compass, for the direction cone), shown only on the device; nothing is sent to the server.
  - Phones draw at 2× rather than 3× and keep fewer tiles in memory. In headless Chrome on an iPhone profile, the page held ~220 MB through playback and panning in 3D.
- **NOAA GRIB2 by byte range:** `backend/snowwatch/maps/grib.py` reads a file's `.idx`, fetches one message with an HTTP Range request (only a 206 answer is accepted) and decodes it with `gribberish` 1.9 (MIT; wheels for x86-64, ARM64 and ARMv7 Linux; needs only NumPy). Re-probed 10 Nov 2026 on `noaa-hrrr-bdp-pds`: `hrrr.t14z.wrfsfcf01.grib2.idx` lists `APCP:surface:0-1 hour acc fcst` (218 KB) and `CSNOW`, `CICEP`, `CFRZR`, `CRAIN` (`1 hour fcst`, 1–30 KB each; `CSNOW` is 5.7 KB, kept as a test fixture). `CSNOW` decoded in 12 ms to a 1059 × 1799 Lambert conformal grid; `metadata.latlng()` returns flat arrays (south row first, longitudes −180…180) in 0.12 s. Mapping that grid onto the West box (nearest cell per pixel) takes 0.3 s and is cached as `.npy` per grid and box. `fetch_messages` reads the `.idx` once for several messages of one file and can cache them on disk. Milestone 2 removed the IEM HRRR tiles and the WPC polygons.
